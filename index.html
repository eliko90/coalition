HOW THIS WORKS

Israel elects all 120 members of its parliament, the Knesset, by national party-list vote. No party has ever won a majority on its own — governing takes a coalition of parties whose seats add up to at least 61.

Parties are grouped into rough blocs (pro-Netanyahu, the "change" bloc opposing him, and Arab-majority parties usually left out of both) — but bloc lines shift, and a party polling near Israel's 3.25% threshold (about 4 seats) can vanish from the Knesset entirely.

The catch this tool is built around: seats aren't the only constraint. Parties publicly rule each other out. A coalition can clear 61 on paper and still be politically impossible — build one below and see for yourself.

Why these numbers

This tool uses the Kantar/Kan 11 poll rather than an all-pollster average. Channel 14's polls consistently show the Netanyahu bloc far ahead of every other pollster and are excluded. At the same time, Israeli polls have historically underestimated the right on election day — so rather than average toward the more optimistic-for-the-opposition surveys, I use Kan 11, which tends to sit slightly to the right of the pack. Treat it as one considered snapshot, not a forecast.
